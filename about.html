A pool of ink that never dries

Drag once across the canvas. A ribbon of color trails your cursor, curls in on itself, then lets go — as if it had never been there. Nothing is recorded. Nothing plays back. Here is what happens sixty times a second, and how to play it well.

01 — Play

How to play it #

Three gestures cover everything. Learn these and the rest is doodling.

Drag to paint

Press and move anywhere. Slow draws a thick ribbon; fast throws it far. Two slow circles beat ten fast scribbles.

Wake the water

Press Space or Burst for eight random stamps. Press P or Pause to freeze mid-frame, again to resume.

Tune the mood

Open Controls. Start with Curl and Ink persistence — they change everything. Reset is always one click away.

Tip On phones, one finger stirs exactly like a cursor. If the pool looks sleepy, it is — drag once and it wakes.

02 — Loop

How the animation works #

Every frame is painted five times. Each pass reads the one before it, like water running through five sieves:

  1. Stir. Your cursor pours motion and color into the pool.
  2. Swirl. The water finds where it is curling and curls harder there. That is where eddies come from.
  3. Settle. Pressure is evened out, so the liquid flows instead of clumping.
  4. Drift. Water and ink both ride the current one step forward, spending a little energy on the way.
  5. Glow. Light, bloom and a little grain finish the frame, then it reaches your screen.

Then the whole thing runs again. No video file, no recording. Close the tab mid-swirl and that exact pattern is gone forever.

03 — Touch

Where the motion comes from #

Your cursor does two jobs at once. It is a spoon and a paintbrush — each move stamps a push in the direction of travel, and a dab of color where you are.

The Burst button — or the Space key — casts random stamps across the pool without the cursor. Pause, or P, freezes the loop mid-frame; press again to resume.

04 — Fade

Why it fades #

Every frame spends a little energy — the cost of the drift step. At the default settings a splash is half gone in about a second, and settled a few seconds later. Raise persistence and it lingers. Either way, it settles.

The vanishing is part of the piece: the canvas is always clearing itself for the next stroke.

05 — Light

Why it glows #

The water itself is matte. All light is added in the final pass: bright areas bloom outward, faint shafts cross the pool, and a little grain keeps smooth gradients from separating into bands.

Turn Glow to zero and the pool goes flat and inky. Push it up and the same stroke turns neon.

Note

On phones the pool is calculated at half size and the screen is capped — the same water, just painted with broader strokes, so it stays smooth in your pocket.

07 — Home

Where does it run? #

Entirely on your own device — the water is calculated on your graphics chip, frame by frame, as you watch. Nothing you paint is uploaded anywhere, and your dial settings never leave this browser.

08 — Answers

Common questions #

Will my painting be saved?

No. Every frame is calculated live, so reloading starts a fresh pool. Dial positions are remembered in this browser; paintings are not.

Why did the page go dark?

The ink has settled — it always does. Drag anywhere, or press Burst, and the water wakes again.

Does it work on phones?

Yes. The pool runs smaller with capped resolution, and a finger stirs exactly like a cursor.

Is anything uploaded?

Nothing. There is no account, no server, no analytics watching your strokes. The whole thing lives in this tab.
