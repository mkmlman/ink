Make a mark #

Move or drag inside the field. Your stroke enters the solver as two things: a push and a dab of color. Change the brush and watch the real simulation respond.

↘ Next, follow the stroke through each pass.

Usage #

01

Paint

Move to paint without clicking. Faster movement gives each splat a stronger push; Brush radius sets the width of the mark.

02

Burst and pause

Burst (Space) injects eight random splats. Pause (P) freezes the flow and ignores new input until resumed.

03

Adjust

The Controls panel exposes ten settings (see Settings). Changes persist in this browser. Reset restores defaults.

Note On touchscreens, drag with one finger to paint.

Follow one frame #

Every frame makes the same trip. Step through it at your own pace.

A simplified view of the solver

STEP 01/ 05

A stroke starts it

Your pointer adds dye at the brush and pushes the velocity field in the direction you move.

Once these passes finish, the canvas displays the updated dye. The simulation repeats this loop every frame.

Input #

Each pointer movement writes one splat: a velocity impulse along the direction of travel, plus dye at the pointer position. Radius and force come from the Brush settings. Stroke color is picked at random for every stroke.

Burst writes eight splats at random positions. Pause freezes the canvas without clearing it; cursor movement and bursts resume when you unpause.

Dissipation #

Advection applies dissipation every frame, so dye and velocity decay toward zero. The default ink persistence keeps the opening colors visible longer while they flow; lower settings clear sooner, and higher settings leave longer trails. Ink persistence sets density dissipation as 2.8 − v × 0.53; motion fade controls velocity dissipation as v × 2.5.

Dissipation is what clears the canvas for the next stroke.

Give it light #

The solver draws matte dye first. Bloom softens bright edges, sunrays add shafts of light, and dithering keeps gradients smooth. These are finishing touches; the motion comes from the solver.

Settings #

Ten settings in three groups. Reset controls restores the starting values for all ten dials.

Brush

input

Radius
radius of each input splat (SPLAT_RADIUS)
Brush force
velocity impulse per splat (SPLAT_FORCE)
Brightness
dye intensity per splat (BRIGHTNESS)

Flow

fluid

Swirl strength
curl or vorticity confinement strength (CURL)
Motion fade
velocity dissipation per frame (VELOCITY_DISSIPATION)
Ink persistence
inverse dye dissipation; higher lasts longer (DENSITY_DISSIPATION)
Ambient motion
background splat rate with no input (IDLE_INJECTION)

Advanced

solver

Pressure damping
pressure dissipation per solve (PRESSURE_DISSIPATION)
Solver steps
Jacobi iterations; higher is smoother and more expensive (PRESSURE_ITERATIONS)
Glow
bloom intensity (BLOOM_INTENSITY)

Example Swirl strength 6, Ink persistence at maximum, Ambient motion at 1 produces continuous flow with no input.

Note

On mobile, dye resolution is 512 (1024 on desktop) and pixel ratio is capped, trading detail for frame rate.

Runtime environment #

The simulation runs entirely in the browser on WebGL2, with a WebGL1 fallback. No painting data leaves the device. Dial positions persist in localStorage (ink:fluid-dials-v1); simulation state is never stored.

FAQ #

Is my painting saved?

No. Reloading resets the simulation. Only dial positions persist, in localStorage.

Why did the canvas go dark?

Dye fades over time. The default Ambient motion setting occasionally adds ink without input. Set it to 0 for a still canvas, or move the cursor, drag one finger, or trigger Burst to add more.

Does it work on phones?

Yes, at reduced dye resolution with a capped pixel ratio.

Is anything uploaded?

No. There is no account, no server component, and no analytics on input.

What if WebGL is unavailable?

The canvas shows a notice and disables its controls. WebGL is usually blocked by browser or device settings — try another browser.
